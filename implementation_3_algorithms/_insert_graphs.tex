\section{Learning on Graph-Structured Data}
\label{sec:graphresults}

% Benchmarks: MUTAG from the GraKeL wheel's bundled test data; PTC_MR,

The two application sections above exercise the walk on tabular and pixel
data, where the graph the walker moves on is chosen by the designer and the
data enter through the coin. The frame of the problem statement is
graph-structured data, where the graph \emph{is} the datum, and this section
tests the walk in that setting. The construction is the one the walk-based
kernels of Rossi et al.~\cite{Rossi_2013} and Bai et al.~\cite{Bai_2015}
use, transposed to discrete time: a walk is run on each graph and
permutation-invariant statistics of its evolution are the graph's
representation. A fidelity kernel $|\braket{\psi_G|\psi_{G'}}|^2$ between walk
states, of the kind used in Section~\ref{sec:kernelresults}, is not available
here --- it is undefined between graphs of different size and is not
invariant under vertex relabelling on graphs of the same size --- so the
statistics are what make the walk usable at all. The classical random walk is
given exactly the same statistics, which is the control that decides whether
the quantum walk contributes anything.

\paragraph*{Data and protocol.}
Four public benchmarks are used, chosen to span the standard graph-kernel
test-bed: MUTAG (188 nitroaromatic compounds, mutagenic or not; $125/63$),
PTC\_MR (344 compounds, carcinogenic or not in male rats; $152/192$),
PROTEINS (1113 protein graphs, enzyme or not; $450/663$) and IMDB-BINARY
(1000 ego-networks of actors, genre; $500/500$)~\cite{Shervashidze_2011,
Xu_2019}. MUTAG was obtained from the test data bundled with the GraKeL
package and the other three from the dataset bundle of Xu et al.'s GIN
repository; the TU~Dortmund collection itself was not reachable from the
simulation environment. Three synthetic binary tasks are kept as controlled
checks, each with one hundred graphs per class on eight to twelve vertices and
edge counts matched between classes: random $3$-regular graphs against
Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graphs of the same edge count (\emph{regular}), random
bipartite graphs against the same (\emph{bipartite}), and two-block
stochastic block models with $p_{\mathrm{in}} = 0.7$, $p_{\mathrm{out}} =
0.1$ against Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graphs of the same expected density
(\emph{community}).

The coined walk uses the Grover coin and the flip-flop shift of
Section~\ref{sec:dtqw-graphs}, starts in the uniform superposition over
arcs, and runs for $T = 8$ steps; at each step the position marginal $p_t$
yields its Shannon entropy, its inverse participation ratio and its maximum
(each scaled by $n$), and $n$ further walks started at each vertex yield the
mean return probability. The $32$-dimensional signature is standardised and
fed to an RBF support vector machine; the classical control replaces the
quantum walk by the lazy random walk and the return probability by
$\operatorname{tr}(P^t)/n$. The walk is blind to vertex labels throughout.
Two further baselines are the Weisfeiler--Lehman subtree
kernel~\cite{Shervashidze_2011} with three refinement rounds, which does use
the vertex labels where a dataset has them, and an RBF kernel on hand-made
statistics (degree histogram, average clustering, spectral radius, smallest
adjacency eigenvalue and density). The walk network of the second experiment
is the same walk with one trainable coin angle per step, $C_t =
\cos\theta_t\,\mathbb{I} - i\sin\theta_t\,G$, followed by a logistic
read-out of the signature and trained end to end with COBYLA; its control
fixes every $\theta_t$ at the Grover value. It is run on the synthetic sets,
MUTAG and PTC\_MR; on the two sets of a thousand graphs the derivative-free
training was not affordable and only the kernels are reported. All figures
are the mean and standard deviation of test accuracy over three repeats of
stratified ten-fold cross-validation, with the SVM grid chosen by an inner
three-fold search.

\paragraph*{On the benchmarks the walk signature matches the field and does
not beat it.}
Table~\ref{tab:graph} and Figure~\ref{fig:graphacc} give the result. On
MUTAG the walk-signature kernel reaches $84.2 \pm 8.8$ per cent, on PTC\_MR
$57.0 \pm 7.0$, on PROTEINS $74.6 \pm 3.7$ and on IMDB-BINARY $70.6 \pm
3.5$. Every one of these is within the range the graph-kernel literature
reports for the WL subtree kernel on the same data, and within one standard
deviation of the WL kernel as implemented here ($82.0$, $60.3$, $75.0$ and
$71.0$), which is itself in line with the published figures --- a check that
the protocol is sound. The label-blind walk therefore recovers, from the walk
dynamics alone, what the WL kernel recovers from vertex labels and
neighbourhood refinement; on MUTAG it does so with a two-point margin, on
PTC\_MR it trails by three. But the classical random-walk signature with the
same four statistics does the same: $87.2$, $58.5$, $74.8$ and $70.6$. On
MUTAG the classical walk is three points \emph{ahead} of the quantum walk,
and on the other three the two are indistinguishable. The hand-made
statistics are likewise within noise everywhere except PTC\_MR. The honest
summary of the benchmark block is that the walk signature is a competent
graph representation and that the quantum walk is not a better one than the
classical walk on any of the four datasets.

\paragraph*{On the synthetic tasks the quantum walk edges the classical one,
within noise.}
The regular task is trivial and every method is at or near $100$ per cent; it
is a sanity check only. On the bipartite task the quantum signature reaches
$99.5 \pm 1.5$ against the classical walk's $97.7 \pm 3.3$, and on the
community task $83.7 \pm 9.3$ against $82.7 \pm 7.3$: ahead on both, by less
than one fold standard deviation. The WL kernel fails on both ($70.8$ and
$64.8$), as expected on unlabelled graphs whose degree sequences are matched
by construction. One mechanistic remark is worth recording. Before the return
probability was added to the signature the walk kernels scored in the low
seventies on the bipartite task, well behind the spectral baseline; the
entropy, participation and maximum of a walk started in the uniform arc state
barely distinguish a bipartite graph from a random one of the same density,
and the return probability, a spectral quantity, does. Which statistics are
taken from the walk matters as much as which walk is run.

\paragraph*{Training the coin adds nothing.}
The walk network with fixed Grover coins reaches $88.1 \pm 8.2$ on MUTAG,
$57.5 \pm 4.9$ on PTC\_MR and $84.0 \pm 7.8$ on the community task; with the
eight coin angles trained end to end it reaches $88.3 \pm 7.0$, $57.6 \pm
6.6$ and $83.8 \pm 8.1$. The differences are a fraction of a point against
fold standard deviations of five to eight. On MUTAG the walk network with a
logistic read-out is the best method in the table, a point above the
classical-walk kernel and six above WL, but the fixed-coin control is equally
good, so the credit belongs to the read-out on the walk signature and not to
the trainable coin. This is consistent with Section~\ref{sec:qnnresults}: the
additional coin freedom enlarges the search space without enlarging the set of
reachable behaviours enough to pay for it. The optimiser had sixty
evaluations per fold from a start near the Grover point, so a better-trained
coin is not excluded; a fairer statement is that on these graphs the
eight-step Grover walk already extracts what the read-out can use.

\paragraph*{What this does and does not show.}
The section establishes that a coined discrete-time walk, run with the same
tooling as the rest of the thesis, gives a graph representation that is
competitive with the standard graph kernels on four public benchmarks and
better than the unlabelled subtree kernel on structural synthetic tasks. It
does not establish an advantage for the quantum walk over the classical walk
as a feature extractor: on the benchmarks the two are within noise, and on
MUTAG the classical walk is ahead. This is the result the benchmarking
literature of Section~\ref{sec:lr-bench} would predict, and it is reported
as such. The kernels here are, as in the published quantum-walk kernels,
classical kernels on walk-derived invariants; nothing in the construction
requires a quantum device, the walk being an exactly simulated unitary of
dimension $nd$ on graphs of at most a few hundred vertices. What the walk
buys, on this evidence, is a label-free representation that reaches the
labelled WL kernel's accuracy --- and the classical walk buys the same.
